# Writing a rule module

*How the engine's expansion modules are built. The design is in [expansions.md](expansions.md), and the flagship mix in [all-hands.md](all-hands.md).*

## Where things live

- `src/engine/modules/<id>/`: the module. `index.ts` exports its `RuleModule`, and `types.ts` its state, actions and events. Split the rest however reads best.
- `src/engine/modules/api.ts`: the contract (hooks, bot, invariants, `rulesVersion`). Read it first.
- `src/engine/modules/shared.ts`: what one module may read or change of another's state:
  - conquest, the corsair and the event die;
  - taking a raider prisoner;
  - a ship back to its supply, where a ship may go, and putting one down;
  - how much oil a hand may still hold.
- `src/engine/maps/`: the maps. `MAP_INFO` gives each its name, a line about it, what its sea is played for, the modules it needs and its own target. The drawn maps are pictures in `atlas.ts`, built into a board by `outline.ts` (which also grows the wild island); the archipelago is generated by `archipelago.ts`. Every map in a pool takes every module ([maps.md](maps.md)).
  - Each module's `shapeMap` hook places its features on the home island by the home island's rules (rule 10; the castle on its corner, the rivers as stamps, the grounds spread round, with `maps/place.ts`), on every map the game deals and on a player's own map where it doesn't carry them. `maps/shape.ts` holds the shared tools: finding the home island and its coast, the hexes features already hold, rule 5's moves and a fresh deal of an island's numbers.
  - A game's board is dealt once, at set-up, by `dealBoard` (`setup.ts`, with `dealSpec` in `maps/deal.ts`), and carried in its options, so `createGame` deals nothing (given no board, it deals the same one). The dealer runs the hooks, places the harbours and then anneals the terrain and numbers (`maps/anneal.ts`); `tests/island.test.ts` holds what it deals to the rules. See [maps.md](maps.md).
  - After the shapers, `partReds` parts any 6s and 8s they left side by side, by swapping plain hexes' numbers (never a feature's, and keeping Invasion's coast all different).
  - `createGame` refuses a game whose board lacks what a module in play needs (`missingFeatures` in `maps/features.ts`), and `tests/shaping.test.ts` checks every preset and expansion on every map that takes it.
  - `tests/sweep.test.ts` (`SWEEP=1`, in shards from `SHARD=0/6` to `SHARD=5/6`) plays every mix the set-up page can make, by bots and the fuzzer. The games it has found broken are replayed in `tests/regressions.test.ts`.
  - A board numbers its land hexes first and its sea after them (`buildBoard`). The renderer keeps per-tile tables for the land alone, at most 61 hexes. A board rebuilt from another (`specOfBoard`, `keepOrder`) keeps its ids.
- `src/engine/presets.ts`: the expansions as players pick them, and the presets, targets and grades (see [expansions.md](expansions.md)).
- `tests/modules/<id>.test.ts`: the module's tests.
  - `tests/sim-harness.ts` runs bot games and the fuzzer with any options (`runBotGame({ ..., options: { modules: [...] } })`, `runFuzzGame(..., options)`). A module that changes a card's total passes its own supply, which is laid over the game's starting one.
  - `tests/helpers.ts` sets scenes another module's state reaches into: `raidersAt` for conquest, `corsairAt` for the corsair. `cardsInPlay` gives a scene's card totals to hold its later states to.
  - A scene that needs other modules creates the game with them, so their `init` runs, rather than patching `options.modules`.

## The contract in brief

- **State** goes in `s.ext[id]` as plain data: no classes, no functions, no Maps. The reducer copies it with `clonePlain` (`reducer.ts`), which keeps what `structuredClone` would (an object held in two places stays shared, holes stay holes) and hands anything that isn't a plain object or array to `structuredClone`.
- **Shared state** lives in the core:
  - `PlayerState.hand` holds every kind of card;
  - `PlayerState.coins` holds coins;
  - `routes[edge]` holds roads, ships and bridges;
  - `Building.wall`, `.seat` and `.foundry` mark upgrades;
  - `GameState.wardens` holds the wardens, and every module that places one takes its id from `newWardenId` (`shared.ts`), one counter for all;
  - `Board.features` holds rivers, the castle and the oasis.
- **Card kinds in play** come from `cardKinds(s)`. A module that adds commodities or oil puts their supply in the bank in `init`.
- **Cards that move in the open** go through `moveCards` (`ctx.ts`): a cost paid, a pick from the supply, a card handed over. It emits `cardsMoved`, which the card tracker follows, so bots and table talk keep a count of every hand from public information alone. Its reason is a `CardMoveReason`, and coins carry a `CoinReason`: a new one goes into the type and into its words in `game/moduleEvents.ts`. A theft goes through `doSteal`, which only the thief and the victim see; oil taken by name is the exception, told openly (`stolen.named`), since the whole table hears it named.
- **A decision a venture shows** (a hand looked at, a venture seen) offers its answers from what it recorded, never from the hidden state, so a view redacted for the player offers the same.
- **Actions** are namespaced `'<id>/<verb>'`, and the reducer sends them to `handle`. Validate everything with `assert`/`fail` from `ctx.ts`; a refused action changes nothing.
- **Decisions owed** go on the pending stack: `{ kind: '<id>/<what>', players, data }`. While it's on top, only your module's actions can be taken, and `legal()` must return the actions that answer it. Pop it when it's resolved.
- **The end of the turn**: give `hooks.endOfTurn` steps with a rank (march 10, battles 20, auction 30, disaster 40). Each runs once and may push pending decisions, and the next step waits for them.
- **Hooks** run in `MODULE_IDS` order across the modules in play, and only those.
  - The reducer's helpers modules may call are exported from `reducer.ts`: `built`, `moved`, `resolveSeven`, `enterRobberPhase`, `doSteal`, `finishRobber`, `finishSetupPlacement`, `finishFreeRoute`, `checkVictory`, `loseGame`, `updateLongestRoad`, `handLimit`. `hexBlocked` and `hexGrants` answer whether a hex yields on a roll and what it would hand out, by production's own path (the presenter and the board view ask them, so a blocked hex never glows).
  - A module that refuses a placement in `canPlace` says why in `placeReason`, a sentence for the build buttons (`placeReason` in `rules.ts` asks it). It's diagnostic only: legality stays with `canPlace`.
  - A piece that moves (a ship) is checked with `moveAllowed` and reported with `moved`, so other modules have their say (Rivers charges a coin for leaving a river).
  - Bridge sites hold only while Rivers is in play (`bridgeSites(s)`). Without it a river is scenery.
  - Without a robber, the 7's theft goes to whichever blocker the modules offer. Invasion takes over the 7's theft, so it takes every blocker off (rule 4), the corsair too. The `corsairStays` knob (All Hands) keeps the corsair: every 7 then moves it, and its theft may take from anyone.
  - **Blocker delays (rule 4).** `blockersWaiting` stops every 7 moving a blocker (the fleet before its first attack). `blockerHeld` keeps one blocker off when another module brings blockers in (Fisheries and Oil until the first 7). A blocker sent home goes to `refugeHex`: a desert, else a swamp, bearing no number, else off the board.
  - A theft no blocker made says what made it: `doSteal`'s `by` (fish, the Almanac, a warden, a venture, Invasion's 7).
  - A module that holds the blockers off the board until something happens (the fleet, until its first attack) says so with `blockersWaiting`. The engine then holds back every module's robber step, Sea Lanes' included, so no blocker comes in early.
  - Any theft of a random card offers oil by name too, while the thief has room for it: list the ways with `theftChoices`, check them with `robbable` and `canTakeOil`, and let bots pick with `nameOil` (`ai/robber.ts`).
- **Production** runs in rule 6's order.
  - A building's take from each hex passes through every module's `yields` hook, and production a module hands out itself should too. Oil runs each well's oil through them, and Sea Lanes each bounty field's picks, so a sickness caps both.
  - `afterProduction` returns the players it gave cards to, or owes a pick of cards. Tokens such as fish don't count.
  - What `afterProduction` would hand a player on a given roll goes in `afterYields` as well, worked out by the same function it uses (Oil's `oilOwed` within `oilRoom`, Sea Lanes' `bountyOwed`). Production never calls it. It feeds the supply's income figures (`engine/income.ts`, which takes the base grants from production's own `rollGrants`), and `tests/income.test.ts` holds that estimate to what a forced roll actually hands out in each mix.
  - `productionFallback` runs last, with everyone who received cards (the Learning level-3 pick).
- **Randomness** comes only from `ctx.rng` (or the `rng` passed to `init`/`shapeMap`), and only while your module is in play. Never draw in a query, and never use `Math.random`.
- **Events** are namespaced and plain data. Each one needs words at the table: `src/game/moduleEvents.ts` turns it into log lines, and for the big moments a banner, a sound and a glance at the board. Its switch is exhaustive, so a new event type fails the typecheck until it has words.
- **The interface.** A module's moves and decisions show in the generic panel (`ui/ModuleControls.tsx`) until a panel of its own claims them: the guild board, the venture hand, the auction, the Almanac, the oil track, the fish page, the coast, the fleet and the card picks live in `ui/modules/`. A module whose `victoryPoints` counts several things names each in `game/points.ts` (`SPLIT`), and a `buildingWorth` change in `BUILDING_WORDS`, so the points tooltip says where every point comes from (whatever isn't named there shows under the module's name, so the parts still add up). A piece a module adds to the build bar (a ship, a bridge, a warden, a wall, a foundry) is an entry in `game/builds.ts`: its move type, price, the spot its pick asks for and the checks that say why it can't be built, in the order a player would put them right; its type goes in `COVERED` so the generic panel leaves it to the bar. A test holds each entry's reason to the engine: there is one exactly when legalActions offers no such move. A module's own way of playing a base card (Oil's Hoarder and Invention naming oil) belongs in the base card's dialog, as one more choice beside the resources, not in the generic panel. Every decision kind has a heading in `game/decisions.ts` (`TITLES`) beside its sentence (`WORDS`); both are exhaustive, so a new kind doesn't compile until it has them. A panel lists the moves and decisions it covers in `game/modulePanels.ts`, so nothing falls through. A move made at several spots of one kind (a turncoat's raiders: the hexes they leave, then those they go to; a resurvey's two hexes) is a staged pick rather than a list of every combination: name its stages in `stagesOf` (`game/moduleActions.ts`), and the generic panel and the input layer pick them in turn on the board. Cards a decision takes from a hand one move at a time (an unrest's discard, a dowry, a tribute) are chosen together in the base game's discard picker and sent in turn (`game/handPicks.ts`, `Presenter.actAll`): a new decision of that shape joins `handPicks.ts`'s list.
- **Bots**: `bot.pending` answers your decisions, and `bot.beforeRoll` and `bot.main` get first say on the bot's own turn. Return `null` when nothing is worth doing, or the base planner never gets a turn. The rest steer the base planner:
  - `spotValue`: what a building keeps bringing, in pips;
  - `spotBonus`: what settling is worth once, in points;
  - `handValue`: what a hand is worth to the module's plans;
  - `freeRoute`: a free piece other than a road;
  - `setupRoute`: a first piece other than the base road (a starting ship), given the best road's score.
- **Difficulty in the modules**: the profile (`ai/profiles.ts`) says how loosely a level plays them. `moduleSkip` is the chance a `main` or `beforeRoll` wish is passed over, `moduleSlip` the chance a `pending` question gets any legal answer, and `moduleNoise` the spread of `ctx.fuzz(key)`, which a judge multiplies into the worth it gives a card or a place (easy bots are wide of the mark, hard bots exact); `moduleSearch` lets the win search try the module's moves (list them in `MODULE_MOVES`, `ai/search.ts`, only if their outcome is known to the bot). A new judge that compares options should take `ctx.fuzz`.
- **Invariants**: `invariants(s)` lists anything inconsistent (pieces not conserved, counters out of range). The harness checks it after every simulated action.

## Versions

A saved game is its seed and its actions, replayed, so a change that would alter a recorded game must keep the old behaviour for the games recorded under it. Two numbers say which code a game was created under, and each covers its own part:

- **`RuleModule.rulesVersion`** covers the module's own code: `init`, hooks, `shapeMap`, `handle` (its actions) and `legal`. It starts at 1 and is stamped per module in `options.moduleRules` (and in saves), for the modules in play and no others.
- **`RULES_VERSION`** (`engine/constants.ts`, stamped as `options.rules`) covers everything else: the core reducer and the helpers modules share (`shared.ts`).
- **Neither covers how a game is dealt.** Every game carries its board (`GameOptions.board`), so the dealer, the maps and the `shapeMap` hooks can change without a bump.

So a bump to one module's version touches only the games that have it, while a bump to `RULES_VERSION` touches every game. When unsure which a change is, ask which games could now play differently.

Every module is at version 1 except Guilds, at 2 (Forge's ordered promotion pairs), and `RULES_VERSION` is 3 (discard choices sampled from public data rather than the hidden dice stream). A redacted view must offer each seat the same choices as the full state: `tests/redaction.test.ts` checks it from every seat through bot games in several mixes, so a choice that reads a hidden hand fails there.

Bump a module's `rulesVersion` when a change to its code would alter a recorded game, and read `s.options.moduleRules[id]` where the code changes, keeping the old branch, the way `beginnerSet` reads `options.rules`. Don't bump for what leaves recorded games as they were: a rename, a new test, a bot that chooses differently (the history records what the bots did, and replay only asks a bot again to rebuild its memory). `createGame` gives a new game each module's current version, `validateOptions` refuses a version the module never had, a stamp for a module not in play, or a module in play without one, and `validSave` holds a save to the same. An expansion save without its stamp is dropped on load. Players hold saves under every current version, so a change that alters a recorded game without a bump breaks theirs.

## The bar for a module

1. **Rules**: the module's rules as written in `docs/expansions.md` and `docs/all-hands.md`, with every number (costs, counts, limits) stated there and kept in the module's constants.
2. **Tests** (`tests/modules/<id>.test.ts`): focused tests of each rule, built from small patched states (`tests/helpers.ts`), plus edge cases.
3. **Bots**: they play it competently enough that a game finishes. A bot-only simulation of at least 40 games across three and four seats finishes every game, with zero fall-backs (`res.fallbacks === 0`) and every invariant intact.
4. **Fuzzing**: a fuzz run where every action `legalActions` offers applies successfully.
5. **The base game is untouched**: `npx vitest run` passes, including `tests/golden.test.ts` (120 recorded base games, replayed exactly), and `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` and `npx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.server.json` are clean.

## Conventions

- A module's code stays in its own directory, its tests and, where needed, `src/engine/maps/`. A new hook or a core change stays minimal and generic, and is documented in `api.ts`.
- **Names and voice**: use the game's own words (the Words table in `docs/expansions.md`), one meaning each. Comments explain why, in full sentences, in British spelling; identifiers are American (`color`). Match the density of the surrounding comments.
- **Commits**: one lowercase line in terms a player would notice, with no type prefix.
