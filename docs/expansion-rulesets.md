# Mixed rulesets

*The four smaller presets on the set-up page (`PRESETS` in `src/engine/presets.ts`): named mixes of the modules in [expansions.md](expansions.md). The flagship, every expansion at once, has its own file: [all-hands.md](all-hands.md). Each preset assumes the rules of its modules as summarised there and spells out what happens where they meet. Grades follow that file: ● settled, ◐ derived, ○ speculative. A target marked "to calibrate" is a starting point for bot simulation. Each preset plays on its mix's pool of maps ([maps.md](maps.md)).*

The four are:
- two settled mixes, the largest available;
- one derived mix that needs no sea;
- one derived mix that takes fishing out to sea.

---

## 1. The Long Voyage

**Sea Lanes + Guilds + Garrisons + Raider Fleet, with First Landing. ● · 3–4 seats · 16 VP**

A home island with a few islets across the water. Towns grow into guild seats while a fleet gathers on the horizon, and the islets pay for the trip.

**Map.** The Sea Lanes maps, the archipelago first: a home island with islets across the water, which hold the two bounty fields between them. 6s and 8s stay apart, and no bounty field bears a red number.

**Set-up.**
1. Each seat places a house with a route piece. Then, in reverse order, each places a town with a route piece. Both go on the home island, and a coastal building may take a ship instead of a road.
2. Starting cards come from the town: one per touching hex, with no commodities. A bounty field gives one resource of your choice.
3. The robber and the corsair wait off the board. After the raider fleet's first attack, the robber goes to the home island's desert and the corsair to its starting sea hex.

**A turn.**
1. Before rolling, you may play the Augury, the Learning venture that sets the dice.
2. Roll both production dice and the event die, and resolve the event die first. A ship face moves the fleet a step. A track's face gives a venture card to each player whose level on that track covers the red die.
3. Production. A town on forest, pasture or mountains yields a resource and a commodity. A bounty field yields resources only (one for a house, two for a town), never commodities.
4. On a 7, everyone over their limit discards half their cards, counting resources and commodities; walls raise the limit. Once the blockers are active, move the robber or the corsair and steal one card.
5. Actions, in any order: build roads, ships, houses, towns, walls and wardens; promote and muster wardens; improve tracks; play ventures; trade; move one loose ship; and act with mustered wardens.

**Where the modules meet.**
- **Ships are routes.** An opponent's warden blocks a lane just as it blocks a road, and splits a lane it stands on when Longest Route is counted.
- **Wardens use ships to travel.** A warden marches along your roads and ships alike, and may stop on an intersection where three sea hexes meet. A warden there may chase the corsair, just as a warden on land chases the robber.
- **A ship can't strand a warden.** You may not move a ship if that would cut one of your wardens off from your routes. A ship's end counts as loose only if none of your buildings or wardens touches it.
- **The fleet counts everything.** Its strength is every town on every island, and the defence is every mustered warden wherever it stands.
- **Bounty fields and the trader.** The trader may not stand on a bounty field, because it has no single resource to trade.
- **Ventures at sea.** The Pilot builds one basic route piece, a road or a ship. Right of Way removes an open ship as well as a road, and a removed piece is rebuilt as the same kind. Tax Collectors moves only the robber, never the corsair.
- **First Landing.** Your first house on each islet earns 2 VP.

**Victory.** 16 VP during your turn (the preset's own target, whatever the map).

## 2. Siege of the Coast

**Invasion + Guilds + Garrisons, without the Raider Fleet. ● · 3–4 seats · 13 VP**

Raiders land on the coast every time the island grows, and the guilds' wardens ride out from the castle to throw them back.

**Map.** The land maps, the classic island first, shaped for Invasion:
- the castle stands on the home island's castle corner (the lower left) and the desert on the coastal hex furthest from it;
- the numbers are dealt each game, a different one on every coastal hex where the coast allows, so every roll can land a raider;
- one raider waits on each coastal hex bearing a 2 or a 12.

**Set-up.** Use Invasion's board with the Guilds' set-up (house first, then a town, with starting cards from the town). There is no fleet track and no robber, and Invasion's own muster deck and simple wardens aren't used.

**A turn.**
1. Roll all three dice. A ship on the event die lands one raider on the coastal hex whose number the production dice show, unless that hex already holds three. Nothing lands on a 7. Track faces draw ventures as usual.
2. Production as in the Guilds. Conquered hexes produce nothing.
3. On a 7, discard as usual, then steal one card from any player you choose.
4. Actions. Every building you place sets off a landing (Invasion's three-number procedure), and so does every **improvement**: roll the production dice once and land one raider at that number (nothing lands on a 7). Coins buy resources only, never commodities, and commodities can be sold for coins at bank rates.
5. End of turn: march, then fight (below).

**Wardens on the edges.**
- **Where they stand.** New wardens are recruited onto empty edges of the castle hex, not onto intersections. They don't follow your routes.
- **Marching.** At the end of your turn, a mustered warden may march up to 5 edges by any path and then rests. A resting warden may walk up to 3 edges.
- **Displacing.** A mustered warden may displace a weaker warden within 3 edges; both then rest.
- **Battles.** A raided hex is freed when the total rank of the mustered wardens on its edges beats the number of raiders on it. Prisoners are shared as in Invasion, and any left over go to the strongest contributor.
- **Losses.** After each battle, one die picks a pair of opposite edges. Wardens on those edges drop a rank (champion to veteran to recruit to removed), and their owner gets 3 coins for each step lost.
- **Prisoners.** Three prisoners make a point (two in Invasion alone). Raiders never run out: when the supply is empty, three prisoners go back in exchange for a VP token.

**Conquest and the guilds.** Town walls don't protect against conquest. A town holding a seat is never conquered: it stays worth 4 VP but produces nothing, and its owner can't improve that seat's track until it touches free land again.

**Ventures that change.**
- Agitators instead captures one raider from any hex as a prisoner.
- Resurvey may only swap among the home island's inner hexes, because the coastal numbers guide the landings.
- The Lighthouse is left out of the Statecraft deck, since there is no robber to keep off.
- The trader is lost if its hex is conquered.
- Tax Collectors picks a land hex and takes a card from each player with an unconquered building on it.
- Treachery puts your warden on the edge the removed warden left.

**Victory.** 13 VP during your turn.

## 3. Rivermouth Fair

**Rivers + Wagon Trains + Port Captain, on one island. ◐ · 3–4 seats · 14 VP (to calibrate)**

A busy, trade-heavy game on a single island. Coins flow along the rivers, wagons gather at the oasis, and the harbours are worth fighting over.

**Map.** The land maps, the classic island first.
- **Rivers.** Two of them, of three and four hexes, run from the mountains to the coast and end in swamps.
- **The oasis** takes the middle of the island.
- **Terrain.** The two swamps and the oasis take three of the island's hexes, the desert among them; the rest are dealt from the island's usual terrain.
- **Numbers.** The swamps and the oasis bear no numbers, so the numbers they would have borne move onto other hexes, which then produce on either (rule 5). All eighteen discs stay in play.
- **Harbours** are dealt round the coast, clear of the river mouths.

**Set-up.**
- The usual snake draft. No road may be placed on a bridge site.
- Each starting house on a river hex earns a coin, and so does each starting road along a river.
- The robber starts off the board and comes in on the first 7 or Knight card.

**Where the modules meet.**
- **Wagons and bridges.** A wagon may not stand on an unbridged river crossing, because carts don't ford. Once a bridge stands there, a wagon may, and it adds 1 to the length of the bridge owner's route, just as it does on a road.
- **Bids are cards.** Coins can't be bid. You can still buy wool or wheat with coins in a trade during your own turn before the auction.
- **Port Captain counts the fair.** A harbour building between two wagons counts at its full worth, wagon point included.
- **Wealth is points.** The richest player's +1 and the poorest players' −1 count toward the target like any other points. Port Captain ignores them, since it counts buildings only.
- **The robber's refuge.** An effect that sends the robber "to the desert" sends it to a swamp.
- **Free roads.** Road Crew and similar effects never build bridges.

**Victory.** 14 VP during your turn (10 + 2 for Wagon Trains + 1 for Port Captain + 1 for Rivers). To calibrate: wealth swings of up to 3 points may need a point more.

## 4. Fishing the Archipelago

**Sea Lanes + Fisheries, with First Landing. ◐ · 3–4 seats · the map's target (14 on the archipelago); the boot's holder +1**

Fishing grounds are numbered water, so they belong at sea: they ring the home island, some of them in the channels to the islets, and the home island keeps its lake.

**Map.** The Sea Lanes maps, the archipelago first.
- The home island's desert becomes the **lake**, which produces on 2, 3, 11 and 12.
- Six **fishing grounds** are sea hexes numbered 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, spread round the home island and each touching its coast ([maps.md](maps.md)).
- The land numbers are dealt as usual for the map.

**Set-up.**
- As for the map.
- A second house touching the lake or a fishing ground draws one fish token on top of its starting cards.
- The robber and the corsair start off the board. Each comes in on the first 7 or Knight card.

**Where the modules meet.**
- **Fishing grounds produce for buildings.** A house touching one draws one token when its number comes up, and a town draws two. Ships beside them don't fish.
- **The corsair fishes too.** It may stop on a fishing ground and blocks it, the way the robber blocks the lake. The robber may not leave land.
- **Fish buy sea actions.** Two fish send the robber *or* the corsair off the board, to return on the next 7 or Knight card. Six fish build a road *or* a ship.
- **Tokens stay tokens.** Fish don't count on a 7, the corsair can't steal them, and they aren't traded.

**Victory.** The map's target, plus one for whoever holds the boot.
